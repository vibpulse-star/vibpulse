Keep your self-employed income, expenses and mileage in one spreadsheet, and have your Making Tax Digital quarterly totals ready the moment each quarter ends.

Built for UK sole traders and freelancers for the 2026-27 tax year. Works in Excel, Google Sheets, Apple Numbers and LibreOffice. No macros, no subscription, no app to install.

WHAT YOU GET
• The tracker (.xlsx), ready to use
• An example copy filled in, so you can see how it works first
• A PDF setup guide (5 pages)

INSIDE THE TRACKER (7 TABS)
• Start Here: set up in 3 steps
• Settings: your business name, tax year and quarter dates
• Income: every payment in. The quarter fills itself in from the date
• Expenses: choose the SA103 category from a dropdown, and mark anything that isn't allowable
• Mileage: the simplified allowance (45p and 25p a mile) worked out for you
• Quarterly Summary: totals for each quarter and the year so far, with each update's due date
• Year End: Self Assessment totals in SA103 order, allowable and disallowable kept apart

GOOD TO KNOW
• Making Tax Digital for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000. It applies from £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028.
• A spreadsheet can't submit to HMRC on its own. Use these figures with HMRC-recognised bridging or MTD software to send your updates.
• This is a record-keeping tool. It does not work out the tax you owe and is not tax or accounting advice.
• For self-employment income. Not for landlords (property income) or limited companies.

For your own business use. Please don't share or resell the files.

## Wording rules (keep the listing honest)

1. Say "helps you organise your records" or "keeps your records". Never "MTD compliant", "HMRC approved" or "HMRC recognised".
2. Say once, plainly, that a spreadsheet can't submit to HMRC on its own.
3. Write "SA103-style" or "in SA103 order". Never "completes your tax return".
4. Put "as published for 2026-27" next to any date or rate.
5. No promises of savings, accuracy or never missing a deadline.
6. No HMRC or Crown logos in images.
